How it works

Find out first. Then fix it.

Most IT problems look like one thing and turn out to be another. I start by finding out which one you actually have, then fix the most expensive part first. You decide at every step whether to keep going.

Step 1Two weeks

IT assessment

I talk to your leadership, your IT team and the business. I look at the systems, the incidents and the spend.

You get: a plain-English answer on whether the problem is the system, the team or the budget, with priorities ranked by dollars.

Step 290 days

Fix the most expensive problem

We pick the one fix with the biggest payoff and get it into production. Your team does the work. I lead, coach and clear the roadblocks.

You get: a working fix, measured results you can show your board, and a team that knows how to do it again.

Step 3Ongoing, if you want it

Fractional CIO or advisory

For companies that need senior IT leadership without a full-time hire: roadmap, budget, vendors, steering committee and coaching for your IT leaders.

You get: a CIO in the room, part-time, accountable for results.

Is this a fit?

Works best if

  • You're a $50M to $500M company
  • IT is slow, unstable or expensive, and nobody can say exactly why
  • You want your team made better, not replaced
  • You want results proven before you commit to more

Probably not a fit if

  • You need staff augmentation or a body shop
  • You want a three-year strategy deck without execution
  • Your team won't have any time to take part

Common questions

How is this different from hiring a consulting firm?

No team of consultants and no roadmap that sits on a shelf. You get one experienced IT leader who diagnoses the problem, then coaches your people through fixing it.

My team is already overwhelmed. Where does the time come from?

The first fix is chosen because it frees up time: fewer incidents, less manual work, fewer emergencies. It pays your team back before it asks for more.

What systems do you know?

Deepest in Microsoft: D365 Finance & Operations, D365 CRM, Azure and Azure DevOps. I've also owned WMS, OMS, POS, PLM, eCommerce and custom ERP. The patterns repeat across stacks.

On-site or remote?

Both. I'm in Orange County, California. On-site for local clients, remote with on-site visits for kickoffs and key milestones everywhere else.
